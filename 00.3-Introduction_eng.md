# I) Introduction
The business world is increasingly confronted with rapid advancements and evolutions in labor law standards. For example, in recent years, the COVID-19 crisis and the mass introduction of remote work are factors that have challenged the definitions and liability surrounding workplace accidents. Furthermore, in a 2022 annual report, the Government of Canada recognized a 10.9% increase in disabling occupational injuries (Government of Canada, 2022) compared to the previous year for federally regulated employment. These elements prompt corporate legal teams as well as labor unions to step up their efforts to prevent and litigate their cases before the courts and tribunals. In this regard, Thomson Reuters (Thomson Reuters, 2022), in a 2022 brief, highlights that 65% of surveyed individuals working in corporate legal departments noticed an increase in their workload. In the same study, respondents expressed regret over a delay in the technology that assists them in their work. While lawyers and judges play a paramount role in the legal process, an essential part of the work relies on research to build arguments for the defense or the prevention of potential infractions. Given the major breakthroughs in language technologies over recent decades, it is legitimate to ask how we could assist stakeholders using tools tailored to their needs. In data science, since the 1950s, an approach at the intersection of linguistics and artificial intelligence has been used to process textual data: Natural Language Processing (NLP) (P. M. Nadkarni, L. Ohno-Machado, W. Chapman, 2011). While NLP originally focused simply on information retrieval, today's research community has developed numerous methods to perform various tasks related to text analysis. Among those of interest to us is document classification via unsupervised algorithms. As a result, various legal documentation search tools in Canada and Quebec, such as CanLII or SOQUIJ, rely on search algorithms based on NLP principles. However, using these search engines can prove technical and complex for legal researchers. The objective of this work is to combine several techniques to offer a search and visualization method for court decisions that makes it easier to find relevant information. As a bonus, we examine whether our methods can help reveal key information that assists decision-makers regarding occupational health and safety standards. While we opted for a representation of the topics covered in the texts via Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) and network graph modeling of the corpus's citation network, we explain the reasoning behind these choices in this thesis. To address these tasks, we must begin by reviewing the current state of research on the topic. From this perspective, we look at both generalist articles on NLP and more targeted articles on legal textual data. In this literature review, we examine the various existing approaches and draw inspiration from them to build our models. Next, we explain our methodology by detailing more specifically the dimensions we must measure and explore to make it relevant. In this section, we also focus on the specificities of the Quebec legal domain and the challenges they raise. Additionally, we review our data collection method, highlighting its potential biases. Following this, we present our analyses for each model. We begin by describing our data through exploratory analysis. Next, we explain in detail how we conducted our topic modeling, cleaned our data, and selected the number of topics. Finally, we present our network graph data to better illustrate its structure. We then continue this work by presenting the results of each method. For the LDA modeling, we examine how the various topics composing our corpus are structured along with several related aspects. Then, we look at how the complete graph is structured, focusing particularly on and interpreting the communities of nodes within it. Finally, we attempt to merge both methods through what we define as a mixed approach. We will conclude this thesis with a section on the limitations and future improvements to be made to this work. This chapter allows us to detail the importance and potential held by data-assisted legal intelligence. Following this, we conclude our work by summarizing the primary insights brought forth by this study.
